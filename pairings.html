Four rules that decide most pairings

Once you know these, you can pair anything in the house without a guide.

Match intensity before flavour

A delicate first-flush tea disappears under an 85% bar. Get the weight right first; the flavour match is the easy part.

Acid needs fat, or more acid

A bright Madagascan bar either wants cream to round it off, or citrus to run alongside it. Put it next to something sweet and flat and it reads as sour.

Salt is a pairing, not a seasoning

A few grains of flaky salt will do more for a dark bar than most partners. Add it to the square, not to the plate.

Serve chocolate warmer than you think

18–20°C. Anything colder and the cocoa butter will not release the aromatics, which is why chocolate from the fridge tastes of sugar and nothing else.

Running a tasting at home

Six squares, lightest percentage to darkest, water and plain bread between each. Taste the chocolate alone first and write down one word. Then add the partner and see whether your word survives — that is the whole test, and it works as well for a dinner party as it does for us on the bench.
